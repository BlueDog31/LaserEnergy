# LaserEnergy - Planning document
## Introduction
  The purpose of this experiment is to explore how the ammount of energy from LASERs of different colours and laser beams sent accross different distances changes.
  Thesis: Does the colour of LASER and the distance between the output apperture and the LASER spot?
  Theory: LASERs with higher wavelength frequencies have higher energy and will output more energy and have a higher temperature.
## Materials and equipment:
* LASER-pen, output < 1mW and wave length ≈ 655nm?
* LASER-pen, output < 5mW and wave length ≈ 530nm?
* Measuring device - Vernier Go Direct Light and Colour
* Measuring tape
* Personal Computer with Vernier Graphical Analysis software installed
* Note book

## Procedure
  First, the distance of 0.1m is measured from the measuring device. At the 0.1m from the measuring device the notebook is opened and positioned so the height of the beam from the LASER-pens is aligned with the Lux sensor.
  Next, prepare the Vernier Go Direct Light and Colour unit for logging Lux in Vernier software.
  A total of 5 distances were measured: 10cm, 50cm, 100cm, 200cm, 500cm. The LASER was pointed into the illuminence sensor, then fired for 10 seconds.
  Both the 655Nm and 532Nm LASERs were fired at all ranges. Often multiple times to attempt to reduce error.
  Once all distances had been measured, the data was plotted.

## Errors
  Humans were aiming the LASERS, innaccuracy occured, especially at the longer ranges. The Point of Incidence, Beam Terminus (POI) would sometimes be just slightly off, causing slightly less light to enter the sensor. During the 10s recording periods, sometimes the LASER beam would slightly move(human error), this causes the large faults in the graphs of some of the test groups.
  The LASER itself had a faulty trigger button, pressing this button with equal force but different finger positioning, would lead to the POI becoming much dimmer.
  There was a very minimal amount of background noise (~40 Lux) which would fluctuate to (~120 Lux). Although this noise is so minimal we doubt it would have any significant affect on the experiment, unlike the previously mentioned errors.
